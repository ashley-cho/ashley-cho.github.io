Data Center Power Atlas

Why this exists

We're pouring an extraordinary amount of power into compute while climate change gets deprioritized. Heat waves and floods keep getting worse, and heat alone now kills 546,000 people a year. That bothered me enough to go count.

Everything here is sourced, and the parts that don't hold up are marked as not holding up.

Assumptions

Annual energy per MW of IEA capacity, as a fraction of 8,760 full-load hours. Pinned by two measurements: IEA global implies 0.388, Berkeley Lab's US figure implies 0.408.
Drives metro readouts only. Ceiling is 0.70 — above that a single metro would consume its whole country. 0.60 assumes the flagship metro is ~85% of the national estate.
For country estimates outside the five the IEA reports directly. CSO Ireland implies 0.698, CBS Netherlands 0.702 — agreeing to 0.6%, the best-measured parameter here.
Epoch's figures are facility power, so PUE is already inside. No independent measurement checks this one.
Future scenarios only. Dividing 2030 load by today's generation is the wrong sum. IEA's base case implies about +21% globally.
Capacity above today gets the intensity that country's own evidence supports, instead of its grid average. On where evidence exists, grid average everywhere else — the four we know are in Method.
Cuts every grid's intensity by this much. IEA expects the mix serving data centers to go from ~40% to ~60% clean by 2035.

Method

The arithmetic

TWh/yr = MW × 8760 h × energy factor ÷ 10⁶ MtCO₂/yr = TWh × gCO₂/kWh ÷ 1000

Each layer has its own energy factor because each quotes capacity on a different basis. Two of the three are pinned by independent national measurements; the third is not, and says so.

No PUE slider: PUE is already inside every anchored factor, because the measurements are of electricity at the meter.

Three layers, not additive

Campuses — 86 named sites with measured facility power. Markets — every operator's live capacity across a metro; a campus inside one is already counted in it. Countries — the IEA's complete accounting, 122 GW worldwide.

Country totals de-duplicate: a campus within 110 km of a mapped metro in the same country is not counted twice.

Carbon intensity

US campuses use their EPA eGRID2023 subregion rate — which is why Wisconsin (637 g) and upstate New York (110 g) diverge. Everywhere else uses national lifecycle intensity from Our World in Data.

Grid-average accounting by default: it ignores renewable PPAs, and also ignores new load arriving with its own gas turbine. The margin toggle prices the second effect.

What this is not

Not a facility census — roughly 11,000 data centers exist; this maps those with public capacity figures. Not embodied carbon — no chips, concrete or water. Not live telemetry; a scheduled job re-pulls every source weekly.

“Announced” is a ceiling of claims, not a forecast. Interconnection queues run five to ten times the actual build rate.
