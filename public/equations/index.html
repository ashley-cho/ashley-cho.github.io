Where they break

Almost every equation here is exact for something that does not exist: a point mass, a frictionless fluid, a gas of molecules with no size, a material that is perfectly elastic, a column that is perfectly straight. The assumes, and where it breaks field on each entry is the reason this page exists. It tries to say the actual regime, with a number where one is known, and what replaces the equation outside it. Non-relativistic means below roughly a tenth of the speed of light. Incompressible means below about Mach 0.3. Dilute means far from the critical point. An equation that says only ideal conditions is not telling you anything.

The failures fall into a few families. A limiting form is wrong by a computable amount outside its limit, and the fuller equation is usually on this page too. An empirical law is wrong wherever the measurements stopped, and often nobody wrote down where that was; Ohm's law holds for metals and not for diodes, Hooke's law to the elastic limit, Arrhenius across the temperature range someone bothered to test. A derived equation inherits every assumption of what it was derived from, which is what the descends from links are for. And a postulate breaks only when the whole theory does, which happened to Newton twice, in 1905 and 1925, and has not happened to Maxwell yet.

The classic misuse is extrapolation from a fitted law: an activation energy measured between 25 and 60 °C applied at zero, a fatigue curve extended past the data, a Hubble constant read at a redshift where the relation is no longer linear. Where an entry knows of such a misuse it names it.

On what is and is not recorded

The name on an equation is rarely the person who wrote it in the form you know. Newton wrote no equation for his second law; F = ma is Euler's, from 1750. Bernoulli's equation as written is also Euler's. Einstein's 1905 paper does not contain E = mc²; it says that a body losing energy L loses mass L/c². Snell did not publish his law, Charles did not publish his, Cavendish found Ohm's law 46 years before Ohm and told no one. The origin field tries to say who did what and when, with the priority disputes where they are real (Hooke and Newton, Lemaître and Hubble, Johann and Daniel Bernoulli). Where a date or an attribution is soft, the entry says attributed to, or leaves the year out, rather than invent one.
