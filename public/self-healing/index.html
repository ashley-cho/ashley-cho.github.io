Two thousand years of self-healing

Every maintenance visit, every truck roll, every replacement costs money (and I hate waste). So why can't we just make things repair themselves, the way an organism does (reverse entropy by opening the system)? This is my note on what has been tried across two thousand years, and where it actually proves valuable.

Two thousand years

What has been tried

In the order it happened, not the order it was explained. Every title opens its deep dive.

~200 BC

Lime-pozzolana hot mixing

Quicklime mixed hot leaves reactive lime clasts scattered through the concrete, used in Roman harbours, aqueducts and the Pantheon dome.

~1,300 years. The recipe is lost after the empire. Smeaton rediscovers hydraulic lime in 1756, Aspdin patents Portland cement in 1824. Both are stronger, and neither keeps a reservoir: Portland is ground fine and reacts almost completely.
1930s-50s

Autogenous healing documented

Unhydrated cement grains rehydrate when water reaches them through a crack, observed in ordinary structures and never engineered into a product.

1960s

Surlyn ionomers

DuPont's ionically crosslinked polyethylene, which reseals a puncture using the impact's own heat, used in golf ball covers and aircraft fuel tank liners.

1990-1994

Hollow fibres releasing repair chemicals

Carolyn Dry at Illinois embedded fibres of adhesive in concrete so cracking breaks them open, tested in small field trials and the ancestor of everything vascular.

2001

Microcapsules in epoxy

White and Sottos at Illinois dispersed capsules of liquid monomer plus catalyst through epoxy, now sold in anti-corrosion primers for marine and pipeline steel.

2002

Diels-Alder thermoreversible network

Chen and Wudl built a crosslinked network from bonds that unzip above 120 °C, which today sells as debond-on-demand adhesive for semiconductor wafer bonding.

2005-2013

Soft self-healing coatings

Polyurethane and supramolecular topcoats that reflow with warmth, used in Nissan Scratch Shield paint, paint protection film, phone backs and screen protectors.

2007

Microvascular networks

Toohey and Sottos connected the channels so agent can travel and be refilled, flown as aerospace and wind-blade demonstrators and never certified.

2007-2010

Bacterial concrete

Jonkers at TU Delft embedded Bacillus spores and calcium lactate in porous carriers so a crack wakes them to precipitate calcite, commercialised as Basilisk for tunnels, basements and canal linings.

2008

Supramolecular rubber

Leibler's group at ESPCI made a network from hydrogen bonds alone, which heals at room temperature with no trigger and creeps under sustained load.

2011

Vitrimers

Leibler's group again, epoxy networks whose bonds swap partners without losing crosslinks, now scaling as recyclable composites rather than as a repair material.

2010s-20s

Battery binders and electrolytes

Self-healing polymers that reconnect silicon anode particles as they crack over cycling, in pilot cells, measured on capacity retention rather than toughness.

2023

Roman mechanism explained

The Masic lab at MIT identified the lime clasts as the healing reservoir, and hot-mixed lime concrete is being commercialised on the back of it.

What the record says

The two fastest deployments were accidents. Roman lime clasts and Surlyn shipped because healing came with a material already being bought for another reason. Every deliberately engineered system took a decade or more, and most never reached a product.

Bacterial concrete is the exception. Concrete tolerates the carrier penalty, and the failure it prevents is expensive.

Deep dives

Row by row, and what pays for it

A crack is an entropy increase. Closing it lowers entropy locally, which the second law forbids only in a closed system.

So every mechanism below opens the boundary and pays a bill. Four currencies:

  1. A pre-charged reservoir. Capsules, lime clasts, spores with their lactate, charged at manufacture.
  2. Imported energy. Heat, impact, sunlight, an induction coil.
  3. Imported matter. Water and CO₂ for calcite, oxygen for SiC.
  4. Metabolism. Eat lactate, excrete CO₂, precipitate calcite. The only currency that regenerates.

Each entry names which one it uses.

~200 BC

Lime-pozzolana hot mixing

Chemical

Quicklime mixed straight into the batch with ash, aggregate and water, rather than slaked first. Slaking releases heat, the batch goes past 100 °C, and some lime survives unreacted as white lumps. Water entering a crack dissolves calcium from a nearby lump. It reprecipitates as calcite and seals the gap.

The clast is a capsule by accident: a dormant reagent, triggered by the damage itself. A controlled process would have consumed it all.

Pre-charged reservoirThe lime clast is free energy stored at manufacture. Regrowing calcite releases heat to the surroundings.
Where it got used
  • deployedHarbour moles and marine worksPortus Cosanus, Caesarea and other concrete breakwaters cast directly into seawater, where modern Portland concrete degrades in decades.
  • deployedThe Pantheon dome, aqueducts, city walls200 BC to 400 AD, unreinforced, still standing. The Privernum wall sampled in 2023 is one of these.
  • earlyHot-mixed lime concrete todayBeing commercialised for durable, lower-carbon construction on the back of the 2023 result. The recipe is old, the deliberate use of it is not.
LiteratureSeymour et al., Hot mixing and the durability of Roman concrete, Science Advances 2023 · Nature Reviews Materials commentary
1930s-50s

Autogenous healing

Chemical

Unhydrated cement grains meet water arriving through a crack and keep hydrating, partly closing it. Real healing, but only for hairline cracks, and only if enough cement went unhydrated. Too variable to specify.

It proved concrete can close its own cracks.

Reservoir plus imported matterLeftover unhydrated cement is the reservoir; the water arriving through the crack is the import. Neither was put there on purpose.
Where it got used
  • neverNo engineered productNobody has sold autogenous healing, because you cannot specify how much of it you will get.
  • designA credited contributor, not a design propertySome guidance allows for it in water-tightness of low-crack-width sections.
LiteratureBacteria-powered self-healing concrete, open-access review covering the autogenous baseline
1960s

Surlyn ionomers

PhysicalChemical

Poly(ethylene-co-methacrylic acid) neutralised with sodium or zinc. The ionic clusters act as reversible crosslinks. A projectile's energy becomes local heat, the clusters melt, the elastomer's own tension snaps the hole shut, and the clusters re-form on cooling. Physical closure first, chemical bonding second.

It deployed at once because healing was a free property of a material already bought for toughness. Every engineered system since has had to justify an added cost.

Imported energyThe projectile brings it. Nothing is stored in advance, so nothing runs out.
Where it got used
  • fieldedSelf-sealing fuel bladders and tank linersMilitary aircraft and vehicles. A puncture closes before enough fuel escapes to matter. Fielded for decades.
  • shippingGolf ball coversThe same resin, in vastly larger volume, bought for cut resistance rather than healing. Most Surlyn ever made went here.
  • shippingPackaging and sealing filmsPuncture-tolerant layers in laminates and heat-seal films.
  • earlyMulti-layer impact-mitigation stacksNASA patents cover ionomer sandwich layers aimed at micrometeoroid puncture in spacecraft skins.
LiteratureKalista & Ward, Thermal characteristics of the self-healing response in poly(ethylene-co-methacrylic acid), J. R. Soc. Interface 2007 · NASA, multi-layered self-healing material system for impact mitigation
1990-1994

Dry's hollow fibres

Chemical

Hollow glass or polypropylene fibres filled with adhesive or inhibitor, cast into concrete. A crack breaks the fibre and the contents run out. Some versions used pH-sensitive coatings, so chloride ingress rather than cracking triggered release near the rebar.

The first system where the agent of damage is also the trigger for repair. A decade ahead of the paper that made the field visible.

Pre-charged reservoirOne fibre, one payment. It cannot be refilled.
Where it got used
  • trialsConcrete field trialsSmall structures and deck sections with adhesive-filled fibres. Never scaled.
  • researchpH-triggered release near reinforcementCoatings that open on chloride ingress rather than mechanical damage, releasing inhibitor before the rebar corrodes.
  • legacyNo commercial productThe output was an architecture rather than a product. Later vascular systems build on it.
LiteratureDry, Matrix cracking repair and filling using active and passive modes, Smart Mater. Struct. 1994 · Trask & Bond, Bioinspired self-healing using hollow glass fibres, open access
2007

Microvascular networks

Chemical

Channels left behind by dissolving sacrificial fibres, then filled, often with two-part epoxy split across two separate networks. A crack breaches both, the halves meet, and the network refills. The first system to heal the same site more than once.

Channels concentrate stress, so channel volume stays at a few percent.

Refillable reservoirSame currency as a capsule, but the account can be topped up.
Where it got used
  • demonstratorAerospace composite panelsBristol and Airbus-funded test articles. Flown as demonstrators, never certified for primary structure.
  • demonstratorWind-blade spar test sectionsWhere repeated healing at a fatigue hot spot would be worth the channel penalty, if it qualified.
  • researchCombined cooling and healing networksOne network charged to two budgets: coolant in service, healing agent on damage. That is one way to pay for the channel penalty.
LiteratureToohey, Sottos, Lewis, Moore, White, Self-healing materials with microvascular networks, Nature Materials 2007 · Trask & Bond, hollow glass fibre composites, open access
2001

Microcapsules in epoxy

Chemical

Urea-formaldehyde capsules of dicyclopentadiene, with Grubbs catalyst dispersed around them. A crack ruptures a capsule. Monomer wicks in by capillarity, meets catalyst, and polymerises into solid polymer bonded to both faces. Roughly 75 percent toughness recovery, once.

The shell must be weaker than the matrix but bonded tightly to it, or the crack goes around.

Pre-charged reservoirThe monomer carries the free energy, and polymerisation is exothermic.
Where it got used
  • shippingAnti-corrosion primers on steelMarine, offshore and pipeline coatings with capsules of linseed oil, silyl ester or a corrosion inhibitor.
  • trialsRejuvenator capsules in porous asphaltSunflower-oil capsules that release when the binder ravels. Road trials in the Netherlands and Spain.
  • earlyConcrete admixturesEncapsulated polyurethane and sodium silicate that flood a crack and cure, sold alongside the bacterial systems.
  • researchElectronics encapsulants and underfillsCapsule-loaded encapsulants for crack arrest in packaging. Not in production.
  • researchComposite demonstrator panelsTwenty-five years of coupons and panels, and still nothing structural.
LiteratureWhite, Sottos et al., Autonomic healing of polymer composites, Nature 2001 · Intrinsically self-healing polymers, Chemical Reviews 2023 · Liu & Schlangen, induction-healing porous asphalt trial section, Materials and Structures

The two molecules doing the work

healing agent Dicyclopentadiene (DCPD) C₁₀H₁₂ · the 2001 core liquid A strained bicyclic diene. Grubbs ruthenium catalyst opens the strained ring and chains the molecules head to tail, ring-opening metathesis polymerisation, giving solid polyDCPD inside the crack.
matrix and agent DGEBA epoxy monomer C₂₁H₂₄O₄ · bisphenol A diglycidyl ether The workhorse both as the matrix being healed and, encapsulated with an amine hardener in separate capsules, as a two-part healing agent that only reacts where a crack lets the halves meet.
2002

Diels-Alder networks

Chemical

Furan groups on the polymer, crosslinked by a multi-maleimide. Every crosslink is a Diels-Alder adduct. Heat past ~120 °C and about a third of them unzip, the network flows into the crack, and cooling re-forms them. Repeatable without limit.

The unlink temperature has to sit above service temperature and below degradation, and that window is narrow.

Imported energy, every cycleNo reservoir. An oven supplies the budget each time, which is what the ~120 °C trigger is.
Where it got used, and what it is actually bought for
  • shippingDebond-on-demand adhesivesTemporary bonding for semiconductor wafer thinning and handling: the stack must hold through processing, then release cleanly on heat with no residue. This is the clearest commercial home for the chemistry.
  • shippingReworkable display and panel laminationA failed unit gets separated at temperature so the expensive layer is recovered rather than scrapped.
  • earlyReworkable encapsulants and underfillsChip-level repair and component recovery, where a permanent thermoset would write off the whole assembly.
  • earlyReprocessable 3D-printing resinsFuran-maleimide photopolymers that can be reground and reprinted instead of landfilled.
  • earlyPhotovoltaic module encapsulantsDesigned to delaminate on heating so glass and silicon can be separated at end of life. Recycling, again, not healing.
  • researchThermally remendable coatings and repairable laminatesCoupons and demonstrators. This is the self-healing literature, and it is the smaller half of the story.
  • blockedAerospace primary structureBlocked by its own ~120 °C unlink temperature, which has to sit above service temperature and below degradation. The window is too narrow.
LiteratureChen, Wudl et al., A thermally re-mendable cross-linked polymeric material, Science 2002 · Prolonged in situ self-healing in structural composites via thermo-reversible entanglement, Nature Communications 2022
2005-2013

Soft coatings

PhysicalChemical

Polyurethane and supramolecular clearcoats. Fine scratches close by chain mobility at ambient temperature or a little above. No load, no certification, and the failure it fixes is one the customer can see. Dates and chemistries are poorly documented publicly.

Ambient energyBorrowed from the sun or a warm room. The budget is small, which is why only a scratch closes.
Where it got used
  • mass marketSelf-healing paint protection filmAftermarket TPU film wrapped over car paint, sold on appearance retention. Volumes are large, though I have not found a figure I would cite.
  • shippingAutomotive clearcoatsNissan Scratch Shield from 2005 in Japan, later on other models. Swirl marks close with warmth.
  • shippingConsumer electronics surfacesLG G Flex back cover in 2013, screen protectors, phone cases.
  • shippingEyewear, furniture and floor coatingsScratch-recovering topcoats sold on appearance retention.
  • notAnything load-bearingNo structural role anywhere, which is exactly why none of it needed certifying.
LiteratureSelf-healing polymers, Nature Reviews Materials 2020
2007-2010

Bacterial concrete

Biological

Alkali-resistant Bacillus spores and calcium lactate, packed together in porous expanded clay. Water and oxygen reach them through a crack, metabolism raises carbonate and pH, and calcite precipitates. Good to roughly 0.5 to 1 mm.

Bare spores in the mix died in about four months, so the carrier is the invention rather than the packaging. This is the only engineered system carrying structural duty, because concrete tolerates the carrier penalty and the failure it prevents is expensive.

MetabolismThe bacteria run on lactate and export entropy as CO₂ and heat. The reagent regenerates rather than depletes.
Where it got used
  • commercialBasilisk healing agent and mixesNetherlands, from 2015. Sold as an admixture and as a repair product, not a research programme.
  • commercialTunnels, basements and water-retaining structuresAnywhere a hairline crack means water ingress and, eventually, rebar corrosion.
  • commercialCanal linings, parking decks and roof decksLarge horizontal surfaces where inspecting and patching every crack is impractical.
  • commercialRepair mortars for existing structuresApplied to concrete that was never designed with healing in it.
  • adjacentCrystalline admixtures and superabsorbent polymersXypex, Penetron and similar do a related job chemically rather than biologically, and compete for the same specification.
LiteratureJonkers et al., Application of bacteria as self-healing agent for sustainable concrete, Ecological Engineering 2010 · Bacteria-powered self-healing concrete, open-access review 2025
2008

Supramolecular rubber

Chemical

Fatty-acid oligomers carrying amide and imidazolidone groups that hydrogen-bond to each other. Press two cut faces together and they re-bond at room temperature, no trigger. But only within about fifteen minutes, before the free groups pair with neighbours on their own side.

The reversibility that heals is the reversibility that creeps. It never became structural.

Ambient energy aloneRoughly 2.5 kJ/mol of thermal energy. That is why the bonds have to be weak, and why it creeps.
Where it got used
  • nicheSoft coatings and pressure-sensitive adhesivesWhere re-forming bonds are an advantage and creep is irrelevant.
  • researchStretchable electronics and electronic skinConductors and substrates that survive being cut, aimed at wearables and prosthetics.
  • researchSoft robotics actuatorsPneumatic and dielectric actuators that self-repair after a puncture.
  • researchSelf-healing hydrogelsWound dressings, sensors and tissue scaffolds.
  • excludedAnything under sustained loadThe reversibility that heals also lets it creep, so this is an exclusion rather than a maturity gap.
LiteratureCordier, Tournilhac, Soulié-Ziakovic, Leibler, Self-healing and thermoreversible rubber from supramolecular assembly, Nature 2008 · Intrinsically self-healing polymers, Chemical Reviews 2023
2011

Vitrimers

Chemical

Epoxy networks with ester linkages and a transesterification catalyst. Bonds swap partners while the crosslink count stays fixed, so the network changes topology without ever dissolving. Viscosity falls gradually and Arrhenius-like, as in vitreous silica, instead of collapsing at a glass transition.

A thermoset you can reprocess. That matters more for recycling than for repair.

Imported energyHeat above the topology-freezing temperature. The network rearranges rather than melts.
Where it got used
  • scalingRecyclable epoxy composite partsSeveral startups now sell vitrimer resins into composites, on the recycling case rather than the healing case.
  • scalingWind-blade end-of-life programmesBlades are landfilled by the thousand and that is becoming a regulated liability. A resin that can be reshaped or depolymerised changes the economics.
  • earlyEV battery enclosures and structural adhesivesWhere a bonded joint that can be undone matters as much as one that repairs itself.
  • researchReprocessable prepreg and carbon-fibre recoveryRecovering the fibre intact is the objective, and the resin has to let go for that to work.
  • not yetCertified structureNo qualified structural application anywhere, same blocker as everything else on this page.
LiteratureMontarnal, Capelot, Tournilhac, Leibler, Silica-like malleable materials from permanent organic networks, Science 2011 · Are associative exchange mechanisms desirable? ACS Central Science

The reversible bond families, and what each one costs

hydrogen bonding Ureidopyrimidinone (UPy) C₁₂H₂₀N₄O₂ · quadruple H-bond unit Each unit pairs with a partner through four hydrogen bonds at once. Individually weak, collectively a real network, and it re-forms spontaneously at room temperature. Cost: the material creeps under sustained load, because the same reversibility lets it flow.
dynamic covalent Aromatic disulfide C₁₂H₁₀S₂ · S–S exchange The sulfur–sulfur bond swaps partners at 60 to 80 °C or under UV, without the network ever losing crosslinks. Built into polyurethanes and epoxies. Mild trigger, decent stiffness, and the softest landing between the two extremes.
dynamic covalent Boronic ester C₁₂H₁₇BO₂ · exchanges at ambient One of the few covalent exchanges that runs at room temperature, which is why it keeps appearing in self-healing gels and coatings. Cost: it is water-sensitive, so the property that makes it fast also makes it fragile outdoors.
vitrimer β-hydroxy ester C₁₀H₁₂O₃ · transesterification motif With a zinc or tin catalyst, ester bonds swap partners while the total bond count stays fixed. The network rearranges its topology above a freezing temperature without ever liquefying. This is the vitrimer trick, and it is also what makes a thermoset recyclable.
metal–ligand Terpyridine C₁₅H₁₁N₃ · coordinates Zn, Fe, Co Three nitrogen atoms grip a metal ion. Swapping the metal tunes crosslink strength anywhere between hydrogen-bond-weak and covalent-strong, which makes this family the adjustable one.
dynamic covalent Maleimide C₅H₅NO₂ · the dienophile half Shown alone for comparison with the adduct above. Paired with furan it makes the Diels-Alder crosslink; the electron-poor double bond is the reactive site, and it is what reopens when the part is heated.
2010s-20s

Battery binders and electrolytes

Chemical

Silicon anodes swell and shrink enough to tear binder and break contact. Self-healing binders absorb the motion and reconnect the path. The metric is cycle life, not toughness recovery, which is a metric the industry already buys on.

Ambient energyThe charge cycle supplies the damage; the room supplies the repair.
Where it got used
  • prototypeSelf-healing binders for silicon anodesPrototype and pilot cells with silicon or silicon-graphite anodes, where the binder has to survive roughly threefold volume swing every cycle.
  • researchSelf-healing electrolytes and interphase polymersPolymers that reseal the solid-electrolyte interphase as it fractures.
  • researchSeparators and solid-state interfacesRepairing contact loss at the electrolyte-electrode boundary in solid-state cells.
  • noteEvaluated on cycle life, not toughnessThe work is funded on ordinary battery budgets rather than on a self-healing thesis.
LiteratureWang et al., Self-healing chemistry enables stable operation of silicon microparticle anodes, Nature Chemistry 2013 · Open PDF copy, Stanford
2023

The Roman mechanism explained

Chemical

Lime clasts from a Privernum city wall showed a brittle nanoparticulate structure, consistent with hot mixing rather than slaking. Cracked replicas made with hot-mixed quicklime sealed in two weeks and stopped passing water. Replicas without it never healed.

Two thousand years between the material and the explanation. By the time it arrived, bacterial concrete had already rebuilt the same mechanism independently.

Where it is going
  • earlyHot-mixed lime concrete, commercialisedDurability and lower clinker content are the pitch; self-healing is the mechanism underneath it.
  • influenceReservoir thinking for infrastructureThe result pushed infrastructure work toward mineral reservoirs rather than polymer routes, which is where bacterial concrete already was.
LiteratureSeymour et al., Science Advances 2023
Synthesis

Why nothing on the list carries load

Plot the families by stiffness against the trigger each one needs. The pattern stops looking like history and starts looking like a constraint.

And the bill sets the trade

A bond that re-forms unaided at room temperature has to be crossable by ambient heat. Rates follow Arrhenius, and a useful rate needs a barrier under roughly 24 RT. At 25 °C that is about 60 kJ/mol. A covalent C–C bond is 350.

So "heals on its own at ambient" forces you into the weak-bond regime, and modulus scales with crosslink density times bond strength.

Aside

One man, three of the constraints on this page

Thomas Young, 1773 to 1829, a physician by training. The modulus on the chart above carries his name, though his own 1807 version had units of length. Navier put it into stress over strain around 1826, and Euler had the idea in 1727.

He turns up twice more without a namecheck. The Young-Laplace equation is the ~105 Pa capillary pressure pulling monomer into the crack in the capsule figure. Young's equation for contact angle is the wetting condition behind "an agent that beads up will not wick in at all."

Whether the healing agent flows, and whether the healed material is stiff, both trace to the same person. He also ran the double-slit experiment and worked on the Rosetta Stone.

The consequence

The unoccupied corner asks for a material open to ambient heat alone and still stiff. Ambient thermal energy at 300 K is roughly 2.5 kJ/mol. Two routes avoid that budget by using the damage itself as the energy source: mechanochemistry, and sense-and-respond systems that heat only the damaged region.

One hedge. This holds for single-network materials, where the same bonds carry load and do the healing. A dual network decouples them in principle, which is the first item on that list. Nobody has made it work at structural stiffness.

What I would not claim

Assumptions, and the weak spots

  • Dates mark discovery, not explanation or deployment. Some rows span years because the work did. Surlyn and the soft coatings are the thinnest public record; I would not defend either to a year.
  • The charts are indicative, not datasets. Modulus, activation temperatures and barriers are order-of-magnitude literature values. Within-family spread is wide enough that points could swap places.
  • Healing efficiency is not life. Published recovery figures are quasi-static toughness on fresh coupons. Healed fatigue after realistic ageing is largely absent, and that is the number an engineer needs.
  • Timeline claims here have a poor record. Structural self-healing has been five years away since 2001.
